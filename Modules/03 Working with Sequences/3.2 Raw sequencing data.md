## Sequencing procedures

> [!process] Standard Illumina sequencing workflow
> 1. Obtain nucleic acids (DNA or RNA)
> 2. Generate Illumina library
> 	1. Fragment nucleic acids
> 	2. Ligate adapters
> 	3. Use PCR to amplify the library
> 3. Sequence on flowcell
> 4. Receive reads and their quality scores
> 5. Preform quality control analysis of each sample
> 6. Align reads to a known reference genome/transcriptome, or assemble the genome de novo
> 7. Annotate the genes
> 8. Preform downstream analysis

- Sequencing will return millions of reads with a quality score per nucleotide 

> [!process] Standard Illumina RNA-seq library construction
> 1. Isolate high-quality RNA from cells
> 2. Enrich for mRNA
> 3. Fragment mRNA using heat + magnesium buffer, to obtain fragments longer than the read length
> 4. First strand cDNA synthesis using random hexamer priming and reverse transcriptase
> 5. Second strand cDNA synthesis and marking for strand specific RNA-seq
> 6. Add dAMP to the 3' ends of the dsDNA (A-tailing)
> 7. Adapter ligation (the adapters have 3' T overhangs that bind to the A tails)
> 8. PCR amplification with primers complementary to the adapter sequence
> 9. Cleanup and size selection to remove unligated adapters and primers
> 10. Library quality control

- You have to enrich the sample for mRNA in order to get significant reads
	- Less than 10% of cellular RNA is mRNA, so tRNA and rRNA need to be removed or else they'll dilute the expression of mRNA
- mRNA is enriched either by depleting rRNA or specifically enriching mRNA
	- rRNA is depleted using beads conjugated with oligonucleotides that are complementary to rNRA
	- mRNA can be enriched using poly-A enrichment, which captures the poly-A tails of mRNA molecules
		- Only an option for organisms that polyadenylate their mRNA
- Since sequencing machines only sequencing DNA, mRNA has to be converted to cDNA
- Early RNA sequencing methods were direction-agnostic
	- Both the original and the duplicate strands would be sequenced
	- The lack of directionality disguised ncRNAs, splice variants, and anti-sense RNA
- New tagging methods allow for identification of the sense and anti-sense strand
	- During second strand synthesis, dUTPs are incorporated and selectively degraded
	- Since DNA pol cannot transcribe UTP, the second strand is not amplified

- Primers can contain unique DNA barcodes or molecular identifiers (random, rather than fixed sequences)
- **unique molecular identifier**: a random sequence of DNA added to a primer to uniquely identify an individual (though maybe not distinct) mRNA molecule
	- Often used for techniques with low-input samples (scRNA-seq, snRNA-seq, ChIP-seq), very deep sequencing, and detection of ultra-low frequency mutations
	- UMIs allow you to quantify how many of a transcript occurs in the sample, resolution that is lost during PCR amplification
## Sequencing output
- The raw images output by Illumina sequencing generates a series of basecalls and associated quality scores
	- The FASTQ file contains basecalls and QC scores
	- FASTQ files are an extension of FASTA files, which just store sequences
- FASTQ files are compressed in GNU zip and typically end with `.gz` file extension
- Each FASTQ file has four lines:
	- Sequence identifier
	- Nucleotide sequence
	- Quality score identifier line (+++)
	- Quality scores, expressed as ASCII characters
- **phred score**: a quality score given to a nucleotide base read placed on a negative log scale
	- Higher phred score indicates higher accuracy

| Phred Quality Score | Probability of Incorrect Base Call | Base Call Accuracy |
| ------------------- | ---------------------------------- | ------------------ |
| 10                  | 1/10                               | 90%                |
| 20                  | 1/100                              | 99%                |
| 30                  | 1/1,000                            | 99.9%              |
| 40                  | 1/10,000                           | 99.99%             |
| 50                  | 1/100,000                          | 99.999%            |
- Most modern sequencing machines can reliably produce base calls with accuracy above 30
